# Google Data Engineer --- Premium Interview Playbook

A mindset-focused guide designed to reflect production ownership,
scalability thinking, and business impact.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Role Understanding

### 1. What does a Data Engineer do?

A Data Engineer designs and operates scalable data platforms that
transform raw, fragmented data into reliable, governed, and
high-performance datasets for analytics and machine learning.

Beyond pipelines, the role is about: - Reliability (SLA-driven
systems) - Scalability (handling growth without redesign) - Cost
efficiency - Data trust and governance

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 2. Why do you want to become a Data Engineer?

I enjoy building systems that turn complexity into clarity. Data
engineering sits at the intersection of distributed systems, software
engineering, and business impact.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 3. Why should we hire you?

I combine: - Strong fundamentals in distributed data systems -
Production ownership mindset - Clear stakeholder communication - Focus
on long-term scalability

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Technical & Production Thinking

### 4. How do you handle production failures?

1.  Stabilize impact\
2.  Assess scope and SLA impact\
3.  Identify root cause\
4.  Fix and verify\
5.  Prevent recurrence through monitoring and automation

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 5. Batch vs Streaming

Batch: - Cost-efficient\
- Suitable for reporting

Streaming: - Low latency\
- Real-time processing

Engineering decisions depend on latency, cost, and business impact
tradeoffs.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 6. How do you ensure data quality?

-   Schema validation\
-   Null & duplicate checks\
-   Freshness monitoring\
-   Data lineage\
-   Automated alerts

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 7. What if requirements are unclear?

Clarify early with focused questions, examples, edge cases, and written
confirmation.

Ambiguity early is cheap.\
Ambiguity in production is expensive.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
